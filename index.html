Why this trip matters

The DHDE model already tracks Fukui's own sites — Tojinbo, Katsuyama, Rainbow Line, Fukui Station East — with edge-AI camera counts, JMA weather, search-intent and hotel data. Today's stops are the benchmark side of that model: high-traffic, mature tourism sites where crowd dispersal, multilingual support and merchant density are already solved (or not). Everything logged here becomes the comparison set for Fukui's suppressed-demand and congestion forecasts.

Today's route

Field conduct

  • Buddy up — never survey a service provider or intercept a visitor alone.
  • Ask permission before quoting a business owner or photographing an identifiable face.
  • Keep provider interviews under 3 minutes; keep an eye on the group's departure time above everything else.
  • Charge phones at breakfast; pack a portable battery. Open this app once over wifi before you leave — it then works offline all day.
  • Rain contingency: canal-side spots (Dotonbori) and steep slopes (Sannen-zaka) get slippery — note it as a flow factor if it happens.

How your data reaches everyone

This app has no server — everything you log stays on your phone. At each break (lunch, the Kyoto→Fukui train, or check-in), open the Log tab, tap Export JSON, and send the file to whoever is compiling (AirDrop / LINE / email). They open the Log tab on their own phone and paste it under Merge in a teammate's export. Do this after every site so nothing gets lost.
